Cessna 310Q Takeoff Planner

Know what happens if an engine quits before you push the throttles up.

Plug in the day's conditions and your POH chart numbers. Get your margins, your engine-out climb, and a takeoff briefing you can read out loud. ← All tools

What this answers: Do you have enough runway to stop if an engine fails right at decision speed? Will the airplane climb on one engine well enough to clear what's ahead? What will you brief before you go?

How it works

Speeds. V-speeds come from the 310Q Owner's Manual (Figure 2-1, p. 2-6; Figure 3-1, p. 3-2; Section I, pp. 1-8 and 1-9). Decision speed is 90 KIAS. Below it, with runway left, you stop. Above it, with no runway left, you fly.

Chart numbers. You read takeoff distance, accelerate-stop distance, and single-engine climb off the POH charts in Section VI. The page doesn't guess them. It compares them to your runway and tells you the margin.

Single-engine gradient. Blue line (102 KIAS) is converted to true airspeed for the pressure altitude and temperature, then the headwind is subtracted to get groundspeed. gradient = single-engine fpm ÷ (groundspeed ÷ 60). Compare it to the departure procedure, or to 200 ft/nm if there isn't one.

Abort checkpoint. This is the 50-70 rule done right. You should see 70% of your rotation speed by half of your ground roll. The page adds a 50% buffer to the book ground roll first, because your airplane isn't brand new. If that checkpoint is past a third of the runway, it turns yellow. Past half, it turns red. Rethink the takeoff.

Limits: Single-engine performance in a light twin is thin and the book numbers assume a new airplane, a feathered prop, and perfect technique. Many days the honest answer above decision speed is "we may not climb." Brief that. This is a planning aid for training. Your airplane's POH and your instructor have the final word.

What this is: A briefing script built from everything above. It updates as you type. Edit it however you like, then copy it or print it for the kneeboard.

How it works

It follows the flow of the Owner's Manual: conditions, type of takeoff, speeds, performance, the abort checkpoint, what you do below and above decision speed, and your plan. The engine-failure steps come from Section III, p. 3-1. Blank spots show up as underscores so you notice what you haven't planned yet.
